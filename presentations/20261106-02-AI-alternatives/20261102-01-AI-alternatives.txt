    Colossus 1 power station
    https://www.gem.wiki/Colossus_1_power_station

        Colossus 1 is the data-center campus operated by xAI (founded by Elon Musk) in South Memphis,
        located at 3231 Paul Lowry Road in an industrial area (former Electrolux factory site).
        The site began operations around July 2024 with reported IT capacity ~150 MW and an initial
        grid connection of ~8 MW, which later increased to ~150 MW via the local utility
        (Tennessee Valley Authority/MLGW).

        To support that load (and ramp quickly while full grid connections were still in place),
        xAI deployed a fleet of gas turbines on-site. These units acted as interim or supplemental generation.

        Aerial imagery from April 2025 revealed approximately 35 gas-fueled turbines on site, many more
        than publicly permitted at the time. The Shelby County Health Department eventually granted an air
        permit in July 2025 for up to 15 turbines generating about 247.2 MW, valid through January 2027.
        xAI announced it would remove or demobilize many of the turbines: a statement indicated that over
        the upcoming months the “temporary” gas turbines would be removed, with remaining units relegated
        to backup once the second substation is completed and connected.

        The second substation connection is scheduled for fall 2025 — after which the gas turbines should
        shift from primary generation to backup power. The permit requires installation of Best Available
        Control Technology (BACT) by September 1, 2025 for the permitted units.
        The permit expiry (January 2, 2027) indicates that further reviews/renewals/expansions may happen
        if xAI upgrades or expands the site further.[11][15][12

        The US CLOUD Act (Clarifying Lawful Overseas Use of Data Act, 2018) is a US federal law that
        allows US law enforcement and intelligence agencies to compel US-based technology companies
        to provide data stored anywhere in the world, regardless of physical server location.

    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLOUD_Act

        The CLOUD Act primarily amends the Stored Communications Act (SCA) of 1986 to allow
        federal law enforcement to compel U.S.-based technology companies via warrant or
        subpoena to provide requested data stored on servers regardless of whether the data
        are stored in the U.S. or on foreign soil.
